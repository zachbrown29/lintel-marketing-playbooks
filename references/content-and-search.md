# Content and Search

What to publish and how often, how to produce, distribute, measure, and refresh content, how to pick keywords, how to fix technical and on-page search, how to earn links, and how to show up in AI answers. For a marketer or founder at a seed to Series B B2B software company.

**Owns:** content cadence by team size, the first ten posts, pillars and the calendar, the brief and production workflow, gated content, distribution and syndication, content measurement and refresh, founder thought leadership, keyword and topic selection, on-page and technical SEO, link building, AI search visibility, local SEO (and when to skip it).
**Does not own:** the ICP, personas, and positioning (FW-FOUND-001, 002, 004), UTM and CRM setup and attribution (FW-REVOPS-002, 004), converting visitors once they arrive (FW-DEMANDGEN-002), community and user-generated content (FW-CONTENT-007), paid ad strategy.

## Content strategy, pillars, cadence, and the first ten posts  (FW-CONTENT-001)

**When this matters:** You have published 6 or more months with no written strategy. Topics come from sales or the CEO. Organic traffic or MQLs are flat despite steady output. You are hiring the first content person. Or you have no blog and the ICP is validated.
**Skip it when:** The ICP, persona, and messaging are not written (do FW-FOUND-001, 002, 004 first). Product fit is unproven. Nobody can own it even part time. Under about 500 visitors a month, paid and outbound return faster than content as a pipeline channel, though the first ten posts still work as sales material.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Content exists, no strategy. Topics follow requests. Success is pageviews.
- Tier 2 Building: A written brief, 3 to 5 pillars, a calendar 30+ days out, keyword research done.
- Tier 3 Operating: A 90-day calendar with owners, every piece tagged by buyer stage, a quarterly session with sales, SEO, and customer success.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Pipeline per piece and per format is tracked, and the library is pruned as fast as it grows.

**First build, in order**
1. Write a one-page brief (2 to 4 hours): one specific audience (a named role at a named company type), 3 to 5 problems taken from sales calls and customers, buyer stages covered, one success metric (usually organic MQLs), and a publishing promise you can keep.
2. Interview 5 recent customers (1 hour each). Ask what they searched first, which questions they needed answered, which content helped, and what almost stopped the purchase.
3. Pick 3 pillars: a real buyer problem, a topic where you hold real expertise, tied to the product. Write one substantive piece per pillar (1 to 2 weeks each).
4. Build a 30-day calendar (title, pillar, format, owner, date) at the low end of the cadence table below.
5. With no blog yet, publish the first ten in this order. Buyer-intent topics go first because those readers already want a fix.

| Posts | Type | CTA |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Problem posts, one per pillar: the costly problem in the buyer's words, the cost of ignoring it, how teams solve it today | Consideration (guide) |
| 4 and 5 | "[You] vs. [Competitor]" for the two names heard most on sales calls. Be fair about who each fits | Decision (demo, trial) |
| 6 | "[Competitor] alternatives," including the spreadsheet or do-nothing option | Decision |
| 7 and 8 | "How [role] uses [product type] to [job]" for the two jobs customers hire you for | Decision |
| 9 | "How to choose a [category]," built on criteria buyers named in interviews | Consideration |
| 10 | The objection that stalls deals most (price, setup, security, switching), answered plainly | Decision |

   Review each post 90 days after launch. Write more of what produced demos or trials, then build cluster content per pillar, and add awareness topics last. At 2 to 4 posts a month the ten take 3 to 5 months. In an emerging category with little search volume, write educational posts that name the problem and distribute through communities and direct channels instead.
6. Move on when the brief exists, 5 interviews are done, 3 pillars are set, 3 pieces are live, and the calendar has held for 30 days.

Full build adds: a buyer question database (10+ interviews, sales calls, search data) sorted by awareness, consideration, and decision. Pillar pages of 3,000 to 6,000 words linking to 10 to 20 cluster posts each, with internal links built before indexing.

**Cadence by team size (new posts a month)**

| Team | Posts | Add more only when |
|---|---|---|
| Founder or part-time generalist (2 to 4 hours a week) | 1 to 2 | Briefs are ready and the on-time rate holds |
| Solo marketer | 2 to 4. Start at 2 | On-time rate is 80%+ and every post meets the brief standard. Review at 3 months, add 1 |
| 2 to 3 person team | 4 to 8. Start at 4 | On-time rate 80%+ and last quarter's posts hit their 90-day goal at the same rate. Add 2 |
| Dedicated team of 2+ | 6 to 15. Start at 8 | Go above 15 only with freelancers on full briefs and maintenance on benchmark |

If output falls under the low end, the brief backlog or slow expert access is the usual cause. Fix that before adding writers.

**Decision rules**
- If traffic is healthy but MQLs are low, check whether over 60% of the library is awareness content, then check CTAs on the top 20 pages. Swapping "subscribe" for a guide or demo CTA often lifts conversion at once.
- If the library has 100+ pieces and organic is flat, audit before writing more. Group into 3 to 5 pillars and merge or retire the bottom 30% to 40%.
- If rankings have not moved in 6 months, check pillar structure, internal links to pillar pages, and keyword difficulty against your authority.
- If budget is tight, go deep before wide, write consideration and decision content first, and update page-2 pieces before writing new ones.
- If sales distrusts content, start with the top 3 objections. Reps send those pieces, which proves value without waiting for search.
- If you are hiring first, hire a strategist before a writer. Strategy stays in-house. Writing can go to freelancers once briefs are good. Ask candidates how they ran buyer interviews and what pipeline resulted. Weak answers cite traffic only. Day 30: 10 interviews, pillars defined. Day 60: pillar pages live, 90-day calendar running. Day 90: first performance review done.
- If a pillar has under 30 to 50 pieces, write new. Past 50, updating decaying winners usually beats new pieces (FW-CONTENT-005 owns the ratio).

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hours per buyer-intent post | 7 to 14 end to end, 3 to 4 writing | Lintel guidance |
| Cost per post (1,000 to 1,500 words, fully loaded) | $500 to $1,800 | Lintel guidance |
| Comparison page (1,500 to 2,500 words) | $1,500 to $4,000 | Often the best return per dollar |
| Content share of marketing budget | Series A 15% to 25%, B 15% to 20%, C+ 10% to 15% | Lintel guidance |
| Organic share of MQLs | 20% to 35% by Series B after 12+ months. Under 15% is a warning | Lintel guidance |
| MQL conversion per piece | Awareness 0.5% to 2%, consideration 2% to 8%, decision 5% to 15% | Lintel guidance |
| Deals closed faster with 3+ content touches | 15% to 30% faster | Lintel guidance |
| On-time publishing rate | 80%+. Under 70% means the plan is too big | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Reading weak results as low volume and publishing more of the same. Picking pillars from internal brainstorms, not buyer interviews. Letting the cadence become the goal. Skipping messaging, so the first 20 to 30 pieces disagree.

## Blog and long-form production system  (FW-CONTENT-002)

**When this matters:** The strategy exists but production is uneven. Posts ship with no brief, expert interview, or search check. You just hired a writer or freelancer. Sales needs long-form and there is no template.
**Skip it when:** There is no strategy yet. Nobody owns production (a lone founder needs only the brief). Content is a side channel next to outbound or paid.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Every post is made differently. No brief, no expert input, no checklist.
- Tier 2 Building: A brief used every time, a written workflow with owners, a search review before publish.
- Tier 3 Operating: An expert interview protocol, a 90-day review set at publish, a writer onboarding guide.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Cost per piece tracked against pipeline, AI help under a written quality standard.

**First build, in order**
1. Build a one-page brief (2 to 3 hours): working title, pillar, buyer stage, specific audience, the one buyer question answered, 3 to 5 key messages, proprietary insight source (a named expert, customer story, data, or contrarian view), the best existing piece and how yours beats it, primary keyword, format and length, CTA, and a 90-day success metric. Filling it should take 30 to 60 minutes.
2. Build a pre-publish checklist (1 hour): keyword in a title under 60 characters, meta description of 140 to 160 characters, H1 near the title, keyword in the first 100 words, 2+ H2s on related terms or buyer questions, a link to the pillar page, one related link, alt text, clean URL.
3. Use both on the next 3 pieces. Add a 15 to 20 minute brief review before any writer starts. It is the best quality step in the process.
4. Add experts: a 30-minute interview booked 3 to 5 business days before the draft is due, notes back within 24 hours. Hold a monthly 30-minute briefing with 2 or 3 internal experts to build a queue of insights.
5. Set a 90-day review date on every piece at publish.

Stage times: brief 1 to 2 hours, draft 4 to 8 hours for a post and 8 to 20 for a long guide, editorial review 1 to 2 hours, search review 30 to 60 minutes, expert review 30 to 60 minutes, final approval 15 to 30 minutes. Editorial review comes before search review. One person can play several stages.

Long-form (2,000+ words) needs a 3 to 5 sentence summary on top, a linked table of contents above 2,500 words, a takeaway per section, one section of actions to take today, at least 2 CTAs, and one original data point or example.

**Decision rules**
- If quality is uneven, fix the brief before changing writers. Pull the last 10 heavily revised drafts and their briefs. The usual causes are a vague angle, an unstated reader level, or no proprietary insight.
- If the insight field is always empty, build a queue from the monthly expert briefing, mine the last 10 customer stories for quotes and outcomes, or brief the piece as a reasoned challenge to a common belief.
- If you use freelancers, add 3 customer examples, 2 or 3 product differentiators, the best competing article, and an expert contact to every brief.
- If you use AI, use it for research, outlines, and first-draft scaffolding of cluster posts. Keep humans on proprietary insight, voice, and editing.
- If a piece ranks on page 2, apply in order: more internal links, an H2 phrased as the search question with a 40 to 60 word answer under it, fresher data, a mobile speed check.
- If a high-investment piece underperforms, ask in order: is it indexed, does it rank at all (below position 50 is a search problem), is it on page 2 (an optimization problem), is it on page 1 but not converting (a CTA problem). Rewrite only after all four.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Briefs done before drafting | 100%. Under 90% means the system is bypassed | Lintel guidance |
| First drafts approved without structural rewrite | 70%+. Under 50% is a brief problem | Lintel guidance |
| Expert interviews done for insight-required pieces | 90%+ | Lintel guidance |
| Long-form MQLs in 90 days | 3x to 5x a standard post | Lintel guidance |
| Comparison page conversion | 3% to 12%. Under 2% means wrong-intent traffic or weak CTA | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** A 3-page brief that takes longer than the post. Running the checklist after drafting. Skipping the insight field because it is hard. Treating volume as the fix.

## Gated content and lead magnets  (FW-CONTENT-003)

**When this matters:** You have 500+ monthly visitors and almost none become contacts. Pipeline depends on paid. Buyers keep asking the same question. Outbound needs a warmer second touch.
**Skip it when:** Fit or ICP is unsettled. You are product-led at seed or Series A (keep content open, build a list with a newsletter or community). There is no traffic. The asset is not truly useful (poor content open beats poor content gated). A founder with no marketer should cap this at about 20 hours.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Zero or one gated asset, no landing page, no follow-up.
- Tier 2 Building: 2 to 5 assets across two funnel stages, each with a landing page and a 3+ email follow-up, and the CRM records which asset created each contact.
- Tier 3 Operating: 6 to 12 assets by stage and persona, progressive profiling, stage-weighted scoring.
- Tier 4 Compounding: A/B tested gates, segment-specific assets, attribution to closed pipeline.

**First build, in order**
1. Validate one topic before writing (2 to 3 hours). Ask 3 to 5 buyers which question they wish they had a full answer to before they started, or read the last 20 sales call transcripts. You want a question with no good free answer.
2. Make one asset, not five, in the format the topic needs (a guide of about 2,000 words, a data report, a template, a calculator). Take 2 to 4 weeks.
3. Build a standalone landing page: an outcome headline, 3 to 5 bullets on what is inside, a form for first name, work email, and company, no navigation. The thank-you page offers one next step.
4. Write a 3-email follow-up before launch. Day 0: deliver the asset with 2 or 3 sentences of real framing. Day 3: one related resource, no pitch. Day 7: a light CTA or a direct question.
5. Set the CRM first: UTM convention, a "first gated asset" field, stage-weighted scoring (about +20 bottom, +10 middle, +5 top). An ICP-fit bottom-funnel download should reach sales within 24 hours.
6. Promote 60 days. Above 8% page conversion on qualified traffic, build the second asset. Below, diagnose first.

| Format | Page conversion | Build time | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research report | 12% to 25% | 4 to 8 weeks | Top |
| Guide or playbook | 8% to 15% | 2 to 3 weeks | Top to middle |
| Template or toolkit | 15% to 30% | 1 to 2 weeks | Middle |
| Calculator or tool | 20% to 40% | 3 to 6 weeks | Middle to bottom |
| Comparison guide for active evaluators | 18% to 30% | 2 to 3 weeks | Bottom |
| Webinar recording | 5% to 12% | Low | Top to middle |

All Lintel guidance. A comparison page built to rank (FW-CONTENT-001) stays open. This gated guide is a separate asset for people already evaluating.

**Decision rules**
- If the goal this quarter is awareness, leave it open. If it is contacts, gate it. Awareness content usually does better open. Middle and bottom support a gate.
- If you lack an MQL pipeline, start with one middle-of-funnel asset. It converts to MQL faster than top-of-funnel.
- If you sell enterprise, gate bottom-of-funnel assets first. A top-of-funnel contact there can need 6 to 18 months of nurture. Add role, company size, and use case to the form.
- If your average deal is under $5,000, use email-only forms and offer a trial by the second email.
- If MQL quality is poor, check qualification and paid targeting before the content. Most mismatch is a targeting problem.
- If MQLs arrive but pipeline is untraceable at Series B, stop making assets and build source-to-pipeline tracking.
- If a bottom-funnel asset converts but sales is not alerted, fix routing first. A buyer who hears nothing for 48 hours is still comparing competitors.
- If budget is tight with 1 or 2 assets, promote the best one (for example $1,000 a month) before making a new one. In a regulated industry, have legal review consent text and keep explicit opt-in, an unsubscribe in every email, and a record of consent dates.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page conversion | Under 5% is a mismatch. 5% to 10% test headlines. 10% to 20% solid. Over 20% scale it | Lintel guidance |
| Download to MQL | Top 5% to 12% in 30 days, middle 15% to 25%, bottom 30% to 50% in 14 days | Lintel guidance |
| Cost per MQL | SMB $150 to $400, mid-market $300 to $800, enterprise $600 to $2,000 | Lintel guidance |
| Gated share of MQLs | 15% to 30% early stage, 20% to 40% at B and C | Lintel guidance |
| Form abandonment | Under 15% fine. Over 25% means remove a field | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Writing the asset before validating the topic. Using the homepage form as the gate. Skipping follow-up "until later." Launching ten assets before testing one.

## Distribution and syndication  (FW-CONTENT-004)

**When this matters:** You publish steadily but traffic and contacts stay flat. Each piece is promoted once. Two or more strong assets get under 200 visits a month. You have never tested paid on a validated asset. Content is never repurposed.
**Skip it when:** There is no content yet. Fit is unproven (distribution speeds up the wrong signal). You have one asset (it needs a promotion plan, not a system, so wait for 3+). Nobody has 30 to 60 minutes a week.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: One LinkedIn share at launch, no list, no UTM tags.
- Tier 2 Building: One or two channels on a schedule, UTMs everywhere, best assets re-promoted.
- Tier 3 Operating: Every long piece has a repurposing plan and a distribution plan before production.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Validated content moves into paid under caps and performance gates.

**First build, in order**
1. Pick one channel for 90 days. Ask 3 to 5 buyers where they find content like yours. LinkedIn fits most B2B. Developer tools may need a community or technical newsletter.
2. Set UTM tags before sharing anything (30 minutes): source is the channel, medium is the content type, campaign is a short title. Retrofitting never recovers the lost data.
3. Reuse your best existing asset instead of making a new one: a text post with the main insight, a carousel of key steps, and a short opinion post, published over 3 weeks.
4. Start an email list with a plain newsletter signup. 10 to 20 subscribers a month is normal early.
5. Join one community where buyers talk. Read it 2 weeks. Share your content only when it answers a live conversation, with one sentence of context. Keep a ratio of ten real contributions to one self-promotional share.

**Cadence by channel:** LinkedIn 3 to 5 posts a week (about 40% repurposed, 30% original opinions, 20% gated content, 10% news). Newsletter every two weeks at minimum, plain text from a named person, 2 or 3 pieces plus one original insight. Community: 3 to 5 contributions a week, your own content at most twice. Podcasts: 1 to 2 guest pitches a month if buyers listen.

**Repurposing:** A long post becomes a 5 to 8 slide carousel, a text post, a 200 to 300 word newsletter section, and a sales one-liner. A research report becomes a weekly data post for 4 to 6 weeks. A webinar becomes 60 to 90 second clips and a transcript post. A case study becomes three posts and a one-page sales sheet. Use 2 or 3 formats per piece. Keep 2 to 3 weeks of approved posts banked.

**Paid, after organic proof:** Gated assets need over 8% landing page conversion from organic. Open content needs over 2% LinkedIn engagement or over 90 seconds on page. Set a monthly cap per asset (a reasonable start is $2,000 for Series A and B) and review at 30 days. First test: one validated asset, $1,500 over 30 days on LinkedIn Sponsored Content targeted by title, company size, and industry. If cost per contact is under $80 and contact-to-MQL is over 8%, double it. Otherwise stop and diagnose. Use syndication networks for reach, not for gated conversion.

**Syndication terms:** The partner must add a canonical link to your original, credit the original date and company, and wait until your original has been indexed at least 7 days. Give each partner its own UTM source. Check the page source for "canonical" yourself.

**Decision rules**
- If you are a founder with no marketing help, post from your personal page, not the company page, once each business day (30 to 45 minutes). Link to content in about 3 of 5 posts. A non-marketer can wait until day 60 to add links.
- If production runs ahead of promotion (over 30% of new content gets under 500 impressions in 60 days), pause production 4 weeks, re-promote the best 5 to 10 older pieces with a new angle, then lower the target.
- If newsletter opens are under 25% and falling, send one re-engagement email, remove people who have not opened in 90 days, and add one original item per issue.
- If you are product-led, aim at developer and practitioner communities, point CTAs at the trial, and count trial signups, not MQLs.
- If you want a fourth or fifth channel, first make the current ones consistent and measured. If LinkedIn reach drops over 30% with no cadence change, change formats. Do not post more.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Content-driven sessions a month | Seed 500 to 2,000, Series A 2,000 to 8,000, Series B 8,000 to 25,000 | Lintel guidance |
| Email list | Seed 100 to 500, A 500 to 3,000, B 3,000 to 10,000 | Lintel guidance |
| Newsletter | 30% to 45% open, 3% to 8% click | Lintel guidance |
| LinkedIn per post | Company page 200 to 800 impressions, 1% to 3% engagement. Personal page 1,000 to 5,000, 2% to 5% | Lintel guidance |
| Cost per contact, paid | LinkedIn $40 to $120, remarketing $20 to $60, syndication $15 to $50 | Lintel guidance |
| Contact to MQL by source | Email 15% to 25%, paid 8% to 15%, syndication 5% to 12% | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Starting five channels at once. Skipping UTMs. Making new content when the problem is reach of the old. Dropping links in communities before earning trust. Syndicating without a confirmed canonical link, which hands a partner your search value.

## Measuring content and refreshing the library  (FW-CONTENT-005, plus the metrics in FW-CONTENT-001)

**When this matters:** You have 10+ pieces and no review process. Content traffic has been flat or down two or more quarters. Nobody can name the 3 pieces that drove the most pipeline. Over 30% of the library is 18+ months old and unreviewed.
**Skip it when:** The library is under 5 to 8 pieces (keep a three-column sheet and review monthly). No analytics exist. You publish faster than you can review (cut production first).

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Traffic is seen in aggregate. Nobody knows which pieces earn contacts or pipeline.
- Tier 2 Building: A scorecard per piece and the top 5 refresh targets named.
- Tier 3 Operating: A quarterly audit gives every piece a status and a next action.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Alerts catch drops within days, and refresh choices follow measured returns.

**First build, in order**
1. Pull 90 days of sessions per piece into a sheet: title, URL, monthly sessions, contact conversions.
2. Sort every piece into Performing, Watch, or Retirement Candidate (under 50 monthly sessions, no contacts in 90 days, no clear fix).
3. Retire the bottom 2 or 3. Check inbound links in Search Console first. If it has links, 301 redirect it to the closest live piece. Log what you removed and why.
4. Refresh one piece with 200+ monthly sessions and a clear gap (stale stats, thin coverage, weak CTA). Record before numbers. Measure at 30 and 60 days.
5. Move to the full build at 10+ active pieces.

| Status | Rule |
|---|---|
| Perform | 200+ monthly sessions, flat or growing, conversion above the library average. Review in 90 days |
| Watch | Below baseline on one measure. Two bad monthly reviews in a row means Refresh or Retire |
| Refresh | Used to perform and has a fixable gap. Write a brief: diagnosis, hypothesis, changes, a preservation checklist (same URL, keep the primary keyword, no cuts without replacement, keep links), a target |
| Consolidate | Two or more thin pieces on one subtopic. Merge into the one with more links and traffic, redirect the rest |
| Retire | Under 50 sessions for 3+ months, no contacts in 90 days, under 3 inbound links, no worthwhile fix. Redirect and log |

The quarterly audit takes 3 to 4 hours for 50 to 100 pieces. Its output is action lists. If it changes the production plan by fewer than two items, it is not being used. Set alerts to a named owner: weekly sessions down 30% against the prior 4-week average, download rate down 50% against the 90-day baseline, or a main keyword down 5+ positions in a week.

**Decision rules**
- If choosing refresh or new: refresh first pieces ranking 4 to 10 and pieces with high traffic but falling conversion (fix CTA and offer). Write new only for real buyer-question gaps.
- If a piece loses 40%+ of traffic in a month, check technical causes first (manual action, deindexing, crawl errors), then rankings, before touching content.
- If a piece has links but no traffic, redirect it. Never delete it.
- If two pieces target the same keyword and both sit below position 3, merge them. If they target different keywords and one ranks 1 to 3, keep both and link them.
- If you have more candidates than time: pieces with links, a clear diagnosis, and position 4 to 10 first. Declining pieces with a data gap next. Low-traffic, unlinked pieces last.
- If traffic falls after a refresh, check for a changed URL, a changed primary keyword, or removed content. Those cause most drops and are fixable in a day.
- If you want to delete the whole library, don't. Remove the bottom 20% to 30% and refresh the top 20%. Reset only after a full pivot.
- If you sell outbound, do not retire a low-traffic piece reps use. If product-led, swap the MQL column for trial signups.

**New versus refresh by library age** (Lintel guidance)

| Library | New | Refresh |
|---|---|---|
| Under 12 months (under 15 pieces) | 90% | 10% |
| 12 to 24 months (15 to 40) | 70% | 30% |
| 24 to 36 months (40 to 80) | 50% | 50% |
| Over 36 months (80+) | 40% | 60% |

Libraries over 50 pieces should also do at least 1 maintenance action (update, merge, retire) per 3 new pieces. Over 150, make it 1 to 1.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions per piece a month | Seed to A 200 to 600, B 400 to 1,200 | Lintel guidance |
| Refresh result at 60 days | Sessions up 20% to 60%, conversion up 15% to 40%. Less means the diagnosis was likely wrong | Lintel guidance |
| Library health | 60% to 75% in Perform or Watch. Under 50% is undermanaged | Lintel guidance |
| Retirement | 5% to 15% of the library a year | Lintel guidance |
| Traffic from the top 20% of pieces | 70% to 80% normal. Over 85% means a dead tail | Lintel guidance |
| Tracked keywords in top 10 | 30% to 40% healthy. Under 20% is a depth or targeting problem | Lintel guidance |
| 90-day reviews done on time | 85%+ | Lintel guidance |

Rhythm: weekly, top 10 pieces and on-time publishing. Monthly, organic MQLs and pipeline, traffic by pillar, rankings. Quarterly, the audit, maintenance ratio, stage mix, and a session with sales, SEO, and customer success. Audit immediately if organic drops 20% in 30 days, a competitor publishes a strong piece on your pillar, or the product or ICP changes (within 30 days).

**Where it goes wrong:** Judging by traffic alone, which funds and retires the wrong pieces. Refreshing before capturing a baseline. Retiring without a redirect. Refreshing a piece that already works.

## Thought leadership and founder content  (FW-CONTENT-006)

**When this matters:** Buyers research heavily and you are absent from it. Sales starts calls from zero credibility. A competitor sets the narrative. The founder has real expertise and a distinct view nobody sees. A raise or enterprise push is coming.
**Skip it when:** Fit is unsettled. Buyers purchase self-serve and don't read long perspectives. You have no distinctive position. You can't sustain quality (no program beats a poor one).

Level 1 is category education (useful, not distinctive). Level 2 is a specific, defensible position on a contested question, argued with evidence. Level 3 is category shaping through original research, frameworks, or vocabulary. Real thought leadership starts at Level 2.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Executive posts are product news and general advice.
- Tier 2 Building: Two or three positions documented, a 6-month calendar develops them.
- Tier 3 Operating: A documented process turns executive ideas into drafts with little executive time.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Original research, press, and speaking put your frameworks at the center of the category.

**First build, in order**
1. Spend 45 minutes with the founder on one question: what do you believe about solving this problem that most of the industry would push back on? Write the answer in their own words, keeping their analogies and examples.
2. Write one 1,200 to 1,800 word post for the founder's LinkedIn: the common view stated fairly, your position, evidence, the best counterargument, your reply, what to do instead.
3. After 72 hours, ignore likes. Look for pushback with real reasoning and for ICP-fit readers who recognize the problem. Praise from colleagues is not a signal.
4. Set up a voice-memo workflow (5 to 10 founder minutes) and post at least 3 times a week for 30 days.
5. Then run a 2-hour workshop with the CEO and one or two leaders. List 5 to 8 debated questions, pick the 2 to 5 where your view is most distinctive and defensible, and sharpen each to one sentence specific enough to draw disagreement. Record the common view, your evidence, and the best counterargument.

Extraction formats, least executive time first: a voice memo answering one question (5 to 10 minutes), a weekly 20 to 30 minute call with 3 questions sent ahead, or a full draft the executive reacts to (5 to 15 minutes). Set a 3 to 5 business day review limit with an escalation path. Calendar once positions exist: 3 to 5 LinkedIn posts a week, one long piece a month, one contributed outside article and one speaking submission a quarter. A newsletter works best every two weeks: one original piece of 400 to 600 words and a question that invites replies.

**Decision rules**
- If you are at seed, invest founder time, not budget: 30 to 45 minutes a day of specific posts from the personal page. No ghostwriter or newsletter yet.
- If no position is documented, run the workshop before building anything else.
- If the executive stalls on drafts, cut review to 5 to 10 minutes, use voice memos, and set the review limit.
- If you are product-led with developers as the audience, use technical communities first and make the CTO or a senior engineer the lead voice.
- If pieces keep failing "could a competitor publish this," the extraction is too shallow. Ask for specific stories, such as a customer who tried the standard approach and what happened.
- If the first marketing hire arrives, do not hand over the voice. Have them spend 20% to 30% of their time running extractions and the calendar.
- Judge the program only after 12 months. Good signs: ICP-fit following has at least doubled, outside references rise each quarter, 5%+ of buyers arrive already familiar with your ideas, and sales reports at least one deal sped up by it. Two or fewer means the positions are not distinct or content is not reaching buyers.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ICP-fit follower growth, lead executive | Seed 100 to 300 net a month (40%+ ICP-fit), A 200 to 500, B 300 to 800 | Lintel guidance |
| Shares per medium post | Under 5,000 followers 3 to 8, 5,000 to 15,000 followers 10 to 25 | Lintel guidance |
| Unprompted outside references a month | Seed to A 2 to 5, B 5 to 15 | Lintel guidance |
| Buyers familiar before first call | A 5% to 8%, B 8% to 15% | Lintel guidance |
| Posts passing the "competitor couldn't publish this" test | 75%+. Under 50% means a reset | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Calling product news and generic advice thought leadership. Writing ten posts before publishing one. Launching a blog or newsletter before testing the position on LinkedIn. Playing safe to "build an audience first." Measuring likes.

## Keyword and topic selection  (FW-SEO-002)

**When this matters:** Search is a planned channel and no keyword map exists. You publish plenty and traffic does not grow. A competitor ranks for terms you should own. Sales hears questions your site does not answer.
**Skip it when:** The site cannot be indexed yet (fix FW-SEO-001 first). The ICP is undefined. Domain authority is under about 10 and you have fewer than 20 pieces. Growth is all outbound or events.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: A wish list of high-volume terms, no map.
- Tier 2 Building: A scored set across awareness, consideration, and decision; every planned piece has a primary keyword before writing.
- Tier 3 Operating: Weekly rank tracking and a monthly list of terms just off page 1.
- Tier 4 Compounding: A model that spots rising queries early from trends, roadmap, and sales conversations.

**First build, in order**
1. Mine Search Console (1 to 2 hours): last 3 months of queries by impressions. Queries in positions 6 to 20 with few clicks are the fastest wins. Expect 5 to 15 you can act on with no new writing.
2. Build a seed list of 30 to 50 terms (3 to 4 hours) from four questions: how does the ICP describe the problem, what solution category would they search, which competitors would they compare, what do they search when close to buying. Columns: keyword, stage, monthly volume, difficulty, business value (1 to 10).
3. Score: (business value x 2) plus a 1 to 10 volume score minus a 1 to 10 difficulty score. Take the top 10. If a term's difficulty is more than 15 points above your domain authority, swap in an easier one.
4. Give every piece in the next 90 days a primary keyword before its brief. Keep a map with one row per page and check it for duplicates, including near-duplicate phrasing of the same search.
5. Track your 30 priority terms. Review terms in positions 4 to 15 on the first Monday of each month.

Full build: research in three passes so awareness terms do not crowd out the rest (about 80 to 150 awareness, 50 to 100 consideration, 30 to 60 decision terms). Sort into Active (difficulty within 15 of your authority, business value above 6), Development (16 to 30 above), and Long-term (30+ above, reached through cluster content first). Pick 5 to 10 pillar topics, each with a primary term above about 500 monthly searches. Each brief records the format, angle, and sections of the top 5 live results.

**Decision rules**
- If choosing between a high-volume hard keyword and a smaller winnable one, take the winnable one. A page ranked first for 200 searches a month beats one ranked eighth for 5,000.
- If you sell sales-led, start with decision terms ("[you] vs. [competitor]," "[category] software for [vertical]," "[category] pricing"). They are lower competition and produce pipeline sooner.
- If you are product-led, mix awareness and decision terms, including "[category] free trial" and how-to terms that reach people mid-trial.
- If a competitor dominates the head terms, look for terms where they rank 4 to 20 or not at all, and build "[competitor] alternative" and "[competitor] vs. [you]" pages.
- If content is not producing traffic, ask in order: is it indexed, does it earn impressions for its keyword, does it match what searchers want (check live results), is the keyword winnable at your authority. Fix the first failure.
- If traffic grows but pipeline does not, score the top 20 landing pages for ICP fit (1 to 10). A weighted average under 5 means rebalancing toward higher-intent terms. Keep awareness content and add middle and bottom.
- If over 70% of tracked terms are awareness, cap new assignments at 50% awareness in any quarter.
- If keyword research or link building comes first, do keyword research. Order: target, content, then links to those pages.
- If you are a non-marketer, start with the words customers use when they first call, then check demand in Search Console.
- Tools: Search Console, Keyword Planner, and your own search results cover a first program. One paid tool (about $100 to $200 a month, verify current price) adds difficulty scores and tracking.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword set size | Seed 20 to 30, Series A 100 to 200, B 200 to 400 | Lintel guidance |
| Keywords in top 10 | Series A 10 to 30, B 40 to 100 | Lintel guidance |
| Terms in positions 4 to 15 | 15% to 25% of tracked | Lintel guidance |
| Funnel mix of keyword set | About 50% awareness, 30% consideration, 20% decision | Lintel guidance |
| Organic share of inbound pipeline | 20% to 40% when mature. Under 10% after 18 months means the wrong audience | Lintel guidance |
| Updates gaining 3+ positions in 60 days | 40% to 60% | Lintel guidance |
| Cannibalization | Zero, fixed within 30 days of discovery | Lintel guidance |

Publishing speed follows team size (cadence table in the strategy block), not stage.

**Where it goes wrong:** A vanity list nobody can win. Only awareness terms. One search assigned to several pages. Writing first and choosing the keyword later. Leaving competitor "alternative" searches empty because naming them feels awkward.

## On-page optimization  (FW-SEO-003)

**When this matters:** Pages rank 6 to 20 and have only had a first draft. Titles read as headlines, not for search intent. Pages have missing or duplicate titles, messy headings, or few internal links. Every new piece ships to a different standard.
**Skip it when:** No keyword is assigned (do FW-SEO-002). The site has under 10 pages. Serious technical problems are open.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Title is whatever the writer chose, no meta descriptions.
- Tier 2 Building: A checklist gates every publish, the top 50 pages audited.
- Tier 3 Operating: A monthly review of pages just off page 1.
- Tier 4 Compounding: No page ships without passing the full list, and template changes are monitored.

**First build, in order**
1. Fix titles on the top 10 pages by impressions (2 to 3 hours): primary keyword in the first 3 words, a differentiator, brand optional, under about 60 characters. Write for the click too ("How to Reduce Customer Churn in B2B SaaS (7 Strategies That Work)" beats "Customer Churn Reduction: B2B SaaS Guide").
2. Write unique meta descriptions of 140 to 160 characters for the same 10: what the page covers with the keyword, the value, a soft call to action.
3. Make an 8-item publish checklist: title, meta description, one H1 with the keyword, 3+ H2s on related terms, length at or above the top 5 competing pages' average, 3+ internal links in (added within a week), 2+ links out, alt text.
4. Add 2 or 3 internal links to each of the 5 newest pages from older related pages.
5. Make the checklist a required step before publishing.

Full build: add a live review of the top 5 results to every brief, 4 to 8 H2s, and 8 to 12 secondary keywords. Score the top 50 pages and update the lowest scorers with high impressions, in passes for titles, headings, and completeness (add sections 3+ of the top 5 results cover and you lack). Build an internal link map. For featured snippets, use a 40 to 60 word answer under a heading phrased as the query, a list of 5 to 8 items, or a table, matching the result type.

**Decision rules**
- If you are a founder, fix titles first, then internal links. Neither needs new writing, and most pages in positions 6 to 20 move within 2 to 4 weeks.
- If pages sit at 6 to 15 despite good content, find the failing cause first: shorter than competitors, few internal links, or a mismatch with what searchers want.
- If a page has high impressions, rank 12 to 25, and under 3 internal links, add links before rewriting.
- If CTR is low but rank is fine, fix the title and description. If rank is 15 to 30, fix completeness and links. If there are no impressions for the target term, the page does not match the search, so redo the live results check.
- If the library has 30+ pieces and 20%+ sit in positions 4 to 20, update before you create.
- If you publish 4+ posts a month with no brief template, build it first. It takes a day.
- If a competitor holds a snippet while you rank higher, their answer is better formatted. Match or beat it. A very small team can skip snippet and author-page work until it publishes 4+ pieces a month.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Missing or duplicate titles | Zero. Under 2% over-length | Lintel guidance |
| Pages with 2+ internal links in | 90%+. Orphans under 5% | Lintel guidance |
| Length against the top 5 results | Within 20% of their average. Over 40% shorter is a depth gap | Lintel guidance |
| Updates gaining 3+ positions in 60 days | 40% to 60% | Lintel guidance |
| Founder time per post for the checklist | 30 to 45 minutes | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Headline-style titles with the keyword late or missing. Strong pages with no internal links in, which triggers rewrites when links were the issue. Empty author bylines. Using the checklist three times and quitting.

## Technical SEO and site speed  (FW-SEO-001, FW-SEO-005)

**When this matters:** Organic traffic fell over 15% in 90 days with no change in output or links. You migrated, redesigned, or changed CMS. Search Console shows crawl or coverage errors and no plan. Core Web Vitals fail on over 25% of pages. A site over 500 indexed pages has never been audited.
**Skip it when:** The site has under 20 pages and is not a channel. Growth is outbound or partner-led. A rebuild starts within 60 days (write a search requirements document for it instead). Nobody can implement fixes.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Nobody owns it, Search Console unread.
- Tier 2 Building: A scheduled crawl, an issue tracker ranked by severity with owners.
- Tier 3 Operating: Weekly or daily crawls with alerts, speed checked before deploys, a migration checklist.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Technical checks sit inside engineering, with live user monitoring and an enforced performance budget.

**First build, in order**
1. Set up Search Console (2 to 4 hours): add all four variants (https and http, with and without www), verify, submit the XML sitemap.
2. Review in order: manual actions (resolve first), coverage, Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, sitemaps. Log every finding in a tracker before fixing.
3. Run a baseline crawl with a free tool (a desktop crawler free to 500 URLs, or Ahrefs Webmaster Tools). Export error pages, redirect chains of 2+ hops, and missing or duplicate titles.
4. Fix in order: manual action, anything blocking crawlers (robots.txt), duplicate indexing between http and https or www and non-www (301s and canonical tags), broken redirect chains on busy pages, sitemap errors. Do not touch medium issues while a critical one is open.
5. Set a 30-minute monthly review. Done when the tracker holds all critical and high issues and the review has run twice.
6. For speed, run PageSpeed Insights on the homepage, a top blog post, and a top product page. Remove inactive third-party scripts first (list them in your tag manager). Then convert hero images to WebP with set dimensions, defer non-critical JavaScript, preload the main image, and add a CDN or caching if the server is slow.

Core Web Vitals targets (Google's published thresholds): largest content paint under 2.5 seconds, layout shift under 0.1, interaction responsiveness under 200 milliseconds. Fix the paint metric first. Judge mobile. Google uses real-visitor data, which can look worse than a PageSpeed score, and field data lags about 28 days after a fix.

**Decision rules**
- If you are a seed founder, do steps 1 and 2 only (3 to 6 hours). Skip crawl tool purchases and structured data.
- If traffic is falling, diagnose in order: manual actions, the trend in indexed URLs over 90 days, a Google update date, recent site changes. Name the cause before fixing.
- If you migrated and traffic fell, check canonical tags in the new CMS, the sitemap, redirect chains of 3+ hops, internal links to old URLs, and key pages left noindex.
- If the site is a single-page JavaScript app, or uses React, Next.js, or Vue and content is not ranking, run URL Inspection on key pages first. If rendered content is missing, server-side rendering comes before everything else.
- If speed fails and you use a tag manager, audit scripts before touching code. Test any new tool in staging. If it adds over 0.3 seconds to the main paint or drops the mobile score by over 5 points, weigh it.
- If you need engineering time for speed, build the case from your own numbers: monthly organic sessions, an assumed share lost (label it your estimate), session-to-pipeline rate, deal value.
- If a redesign is within 90 days, skip fixes it will remove. Spend the time on the migration checklist (staging lock, redirect map, canonical plan, sitemap, launch-day steps, 90 days of monitoring). Require search sign-off on any change touching 50+ URLs.
- If you add structured data, do it after critical issues: FAQ on question content, Organization on the homepage, SoftwareApplication on the product page, in JSON-LD, tested first.
- If the blog is on a subdomain, move it to a subfolder unless the CMS cannot. If a manual action appears, stop other search work until it clears.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Indexed URLs vs. pages meant to be indexed | Within 3% | Lintel guidance |
| Coverage error rate | Under 2%. Under 0.5% for a well-kept site | Lintel guidance |
| Good-page rate on Core Web Vitals (mobile) | 75%+ at Tier 3, 85%+ at Tier 4 | Lintel guidance |
| Redirect chains | One hop | Lintel guidance |
| Server response | Under 800 ms good, over 1.8 s poor | Lintel guidance |
| Third-party script domains | Under 10 good, over 20 needs a purge | Lintel guidance |
| Cost to fix one critical issue | 1 to 4 developer hours | Lintel guidance |

Report indexed URLs, coverage error rate, good-page rate, redirect chains, and structured data errors. Do not report a tool's "health score," which can read 80+ while a canonical error costs a third of traffic.

**Where it goes wrong:** Fixing easy issues while critical ones wait for developer time. Calling a migration done when redirects work. Publishing for months without checking indexing. Adding tools with no owner for their combined speed cost. Letting auto-generated tag and archive pages bloat the index.

## Link building  (FW-SEO-004)

**When this matters:** On-page is strong and content is good but rankings stall below page 1. A competitor with similar content outranks you on authority. Your authority is low (domain rating under about 30) and target terms need more. The domain is new or migrated. Decision-stage pages sit on page 2 despite good on-page scores.
**Skip it when:** Technical failures are open (links to unindexed pages do nothing). Average on-page score is under about 60, because links amplify quality and do not replace it. You have under 20 pieces. Fit is unsettled.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: No audit, waiting for links to arrive.
- Tier 2 Building: A 30 to 50 prospect list and a first outreach batch aimed at specific pages.
- Tier 3 Operating: Monthly targets, cadences, and assets built to earn links.
- Tier 4 Compounding: PR, partner links, data content, and brand monitoring with monthly velocity targets.

**First build, in order**
1. Audit backlinks in Ahrefs or similar (2 to 3 hours): referring domains, top 10 linking domains, anchor text mix, spam patterns. Note why your 3 best links happened.
2. Set free brand alerts (30 minutes) for the company, product, and founder names.
3. Ask for links where you are already mentioned without one. Send a short personal note thanking them and naming the best page to link. Expect 2 to 7 links from 5 to 20 mentions.
4. Submit to the 3 to 5 directories your buyers use (G2, Capterra, Product Hunt, Crunchbase), partner and integration pages, and associations. 2 to 4 hours, 5 to 15 permanent links.
5. Make one asset built to be cited. The best first choice is original data: a survey of 100+ buyers (4 to 8 weeks). Alternatives are a "state of the industry" reference from public data or a free calculator. Move on once it earns 5 links.

Full build: a target list of 90 to 135 prospects in three tiers (20 to 30 high-authority relevant sites, 50 to 75 mid-authority, 20 to 30 directories and resource pages). One linkable asset a quarter. Digital PR to 50 to 100 relevant journalists with personalized 150 to 200 word pitches and one follow-up 5 to 7 days later. Resource page, broken-link, and contributed-article outreach. Weekly checks of new and lost links.

**Decision rules**
- If you are a founder with no marketing help, do steps 1 to 4 only and wait for 20+ good pieces before outreach. Your network is the main link asset.
- If choosing content or links: under 20 pieces, content. At 20 to 50 pieces with a rating under 25, both. At 50+ and a rating over 30, links get more because authority becomes the limit.
- If a competitor's authority is 20+ points above yours, standard outreach takes 2 to 3 years to close the gap. Compete on long-tail terms, or run a strong research report with press outreach, which can earn 15 to 40 links in 8 to 12 weeks.
- If an agency guarantees links or sells placements, those are paid links. They break Google's rules and risk a manual action. End it, audit, and rebuild. If asked whether to buy links, the answer is no.
- If exact-match anchor text passes 20% of your links, stop requesting anchors, review recent sources for paid or arranged links, and watch Search Console for notices.
- If guest posting is the question, it works on real publications with editors and readers (authority above about 40). Mass-accept blogs are devalued.
- If you are partner-led, check every partner and integration page for a link to you first.
- If budget is minimal, use brand alerts, directories, partner links, and one original-data piece from your own anonymized data. A dedicated link hire makes sense past about 15 hours a week of work.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Domain rating (use one tool consistently) | Seed target 20+ after 12 months, Series A 30 to 40, B 40 to 55 | Lintel guidance |
| Referring domains | Series A 100 to 250 after 18 months, B 250 to 600 | Lintel guidance |
| New referring domains a month | Series A 5 to 10, B 10 to 20 | Lintel guidance |
| Quality mix | 30%+ from authority 30+, 10%+ from 50+ | Lintel guidance |
| Anchor text | Branded 35% to 50%, bare URL 15% to 20%, generic 10% to 15%, partial match 10% to 15%, exact match under 10% | Lintel guidance |
| Outreach success | Mention reclamation 20% to 35%, broken-link 10% to 20%, resource pages 8% to 15%, PR pitches 5% to 15% | Lintel guidance |
| Research report links | 20 to 50+ domains in 90 days with promotion. Cost about $5,000 to $18,000 | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** "Build it and they will link" at low authority, with no outreach. Pitching big publications before there is anything to pitch. Paying for links. Swapping links with partners at scale. Skipping the baseline audit.

## Showing up in AI answers  (FW-SEO-007, FW-SEO-008)

Two jobs. Answer engine work (FW-SEO-007) gets your pages cited when tools like Google's AI Overviews and Perplexity pull live pages. Generative engine work (FW-SEO-008) shapes what assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini say about you from what they learned. Do the first before the second. The library was written in April 2026 and this field moves fast, so retest platform behavior before relying on any figure here.

**When this matters:** In Search Console, impressions for target queries rise while clicks fall (split by query type first: falling informational clicks with steady commercial clicks points to AI Overviews). Competitors appear in AI answers for your queries and you don't. Ask an assistant "What is [your company]?" and the answer is wrong or missing. Buyers use AI tools for vendor research (confirm on sales calls). You have 50+ indexed pages and plateaued traffic.
**Skip it when:** You have under 30 substantive indexed pages (build content, run only a baseline). The technical basics are broken. Your constraint is closing deals or retention, not awareness (run the baseline, save it dated, revisit later). Positioning is unstable. For generative work, buyers don't use AI assistants (confirm, don't assume).

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Nobody has tested how AI tools answer your category questions.
- Tier 2 Building: Top pages open with direct answers, FAQ markup on question pages, a monthly check.
- Tier 3 Operating: A buyer-question map, dedicated answer pages, full structured data, competitor comparison, matching entity profiles.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Every new piece answers one question directly, original research ships quarterly, quarterly audit across assistants.

**First build, answer engines, in order**
1. Run a baseline (2 to 3 hours). List 20 to 30 buyer questions: category definitions, comparisons, evaluation criteria, problems. Test each in Google (logged out) and Perplexity. Record whether an AI answer appears, who is cited, and whether you appear.
2. Audit the top 20 pages. Mark each major section as answer-first or background-first.
3. Fix the worst 10 pages (4 to 6 hours). Add a direct answer of 40 to 60 words at the top of each section and keep the detail below. Don't cut depth. Shallow answers are cited less than deep ones.
4. Add FAQ markup to question content in buyer wording and test it. Wait at least 90 days before judging it.
5. Retest monthly. Citation rate is queries where you appear divided by queries tested.

Full build: widen to 50 queries (10 awareness, 15 comparison, 15 evaluation, 10 objection). Finish library-wide restructuring in about 60 days. Build a question map from People Also Ask, Perplexity related questions, sales, and support, then make focused 500 to 1,000 word answer pages for uncovered questions, comparison and evaluation first. Add HowTo and Organization markup, named authors with real bios, visible dates, and cited sources. Review stat-heavy pages every 6 months. Compare share of voice with named competitors each quarter.

**First build, generative, in order**
1. Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity 8 questions: what is [company], what does it do, who is it for, who are its competitors, how does it compare to [competitor], best [category] for [use case], which companies offer [category], what to look for when evaluating [category]. Save full answers. Note accuracy, vocabulary, tone, and who else is named.
2. Write one reference page: the exact category name (2 to 4 words), a one-sentence positioning statement, the ICP, 3 to 5 differentiators, and main competitors. Do this before editing any profile.
3. Update Wikidata (no notability bar), Crunchbase, LinkedIn, G2 and Capterra (correct category placement), and your About and Press pages to match. All three steps take about 8 to 12 hours. Re-run quarterly.
4. At Series A and above, earn steady mentions in respected publications and supply your press boilerplate. At Series B and above, add original research and a category definition page. Pursue Wikipedia only after coverage in about three major independent outlets, written neutrally.

**Decision rules**
- If the site has under 30 pages, or key pages are not indexed or not rendering, stop. Run a 10 to 15 query baseline once, then fix the foundation.
- If you are a founder with no marketing help, run only the baseline (about 3 hours), restructure the 5 best pages (5 to 8 hours), and retest monthly (1 hour). Skip schema and entity work until month two.
- If you win featured snippets but appear in under 25% of AI answers, keep the snippet work and add author evidence, topic depth, the question map, and FAQ markup.
- If structure scores well but citation rate is flat for 3 months, the gap is authority. Add named authors, visible dates, and 2 outside mentions a month, then consider link building.
- If you are product-led at SMB, aim the first 10 answer pages at comparison, evaluation, and cost questions, and link each to the trial.
- If you sell enterprise or outbound, treat this as a credibility layer. Restructure only category and comparison pages prospects check after outreach, and test the top 5 queries before each outbound cycle.
- If someone proposes hidden text or pages written to instruct AI assistants, decline. It does not change what models learn and may be treated as spam.
- If positioning is still moving, run only the 8-question baseline. Vocabulary pushed into profiles too early gets redone.
- If FAQ markup "did nothing," check for zero errors in Search Console, direct answers of 40 to 150 words in buyer wording, and that 90 days have passed.

**Numbers that matter**

| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| AI citation rate (share of monitored queries where you appear) | Under 15% not started, 15% to 35% early, 35% to 55% developing, 55% to 70% strong | Lintel guidance. Judge after 3 months of data |
| Informational query click rate | Under about 12.5% hints AI answers are absorbing clicks | Lintel guidance |
| Pages that lead with the answer | 80%+ of the library | Lintel guidance |
| Pages reviewed in the last 12 months | 80%+ | Lintel guidance |
| Appearing in the 8 category queries | Tier 2: 2 to 3. Tier 3: 4 to 6. Tier 4: 6 to 8 | Lintel guidance |
| Outside mentions in strong sources | Series A 1 to 3 a month, B 4 to 8 | Lintel guidance |
| Profile completeness across 5 sources | 90%+ using your exact vocabulary | Lintel guidance |

**Where it goes wrong:** Assuming it needs all-new content when the answers are buried in paragraph four. Cutting depth to shorten answers. Updating profiles before agreeing on vocabulary. Treating one audit as permanent, because representation changes with model updates. Running this before the SEO basics, or dropping classic SEO to chase it.

## Local SEO (usually skip for B2B software)  (FW-SEO-006)

**When this matters:** Geography shapes the sale. Customers cluster in a few metros. You have an office or sales team in specific cities. Competitors show in map results for searches you should win. You are opening a new region.
**Skip it when:** The product is fully remote and buyers in any city decide the same way. Growth is outbound or enterprise relationships. You have under 10 customers and no regional pattern. You sell to developers who find products through communities and documentation.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Google Business Profile unclaimed or neglected.
- Tier 2 Building: Profile complete, listings consistent on name, address, phone.
- Tier 3 Operating: A page per target metro with local proof, and a steady review process.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Local links, local rank tracking, share of voice against named competitors.

**First build, in order**
1. Claim and verify the Google Business Profile for each real location (2 to 4 hours). An unclaimed profile can be edited by anyone.
2. Fill every field, add photos, set review alerts, schedule the first post.
3. Ask all customers for reviews, not only happy ones, and offer no rewards. Google bars selective asking and incentives.
4. Defer listing cleanup and metro pages until a pattern shows.

**Decision rules**
- If it is seed with no regional pattern, do the 30-minute profile and return when 30%+ of customers share one metro.
- If 50%+ of customers sit in one metro, do steps 1 to 3 only (3 to 5 hours) and defer the rest to Series A.
- If your rating is under 4.0 with over 10 reviews, fix the cause and reply to complaints before paying for visibility.
- If a competitor has 3x your reviews, put all local effort into the review process first.
- If metro pages only swap the city name, improve the top 3 with a local customer reference, local rules or market context, and a local association before building more.
- If you open a new market within 60 days, start now. Local signals take 60 to 90 days to count.

**Where it goes wrong:** Writing it off when geography matters, or calling a claimed profile a program. Driving traffic to a low-rated listing. Duplicate city pages. Asking only happy customers for reviews.
