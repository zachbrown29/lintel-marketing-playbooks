# Channels: social, events, community, podcast, influence

Which reach-and-relationship channel to start and when, what each needs in hours and money, and the numbers that say keep going or stop. For a marketer or founder at a seed to Series B B2B software company.

**Owns:** which of these channels to start first; capacity and cadence rules; social cadence and measurement; event selection, sponsorship go/no-go and the return target; owned events and executive dinners; webinars and show rate; community build, hours, and health; podcast economics; creator programs; analyst briefings; customer references.
**Does not own:** paid social and paid LinkedIn (FW-DEMANDGEN-006); content strategy, ghost-writing, and distribution (FW-CONTENT-001, FW-CONTENT-006); brand voice (FW-BRAND-003); account-based programs (FW-EMERGING-003); category creation and the analyst story (FW-EMERGING-005, FW-PM-007); press (FW-PR-001); the measurement base and CRM source fields (FW-FOUND-006); retention programs beyond community and references (FW-CX series).

Source note: analyst relations comes from FW-INFLUENCE-001 and references from FW-INFLUENCE-003. Creators come from FW-EMERGING-007, which the library names as the owner of creators and influencers. The library has no FW-INFLUENCE-002.

## Which channel to start, and when  (cross-channel; each block below owns its detail)

**When this matters:** Hours are limited and several channels are asking for them.
**Skip it when:** One channel is already running at Tier 3 and the question is about that channel only.

| Channel | Do not start until | Time to results | Hours needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder LinkedIn | PMF is plausible and the founder has real expertise | 3 to 6 months | 2 to 3 a week |
| Company social | ICP defined, PMF confirmed, a named owner | 3 to 9 months to pipeline | 4 to 6 a week at Series A |
| Attend and speak at events | A defined ICP and someone to follow up within 48 hours | Inquiries in days, pipeline at 90 days | Travel and prep |
| Sponsored events | CRM event tags, a follow-up owner, a pipeline target per event | Pipeline read at 90 days | 8 to 12 hours to build measurement |
| Webinars | Content program, ICP, CRM tags | Pipeline read at 90 days | 15 to 25% of one marketer |
| Executive dinners | 25 or more customers; CEO knows 15 or more ICP executives | Pipeline read at 90 days | About 4 CEO hours per dinner |
| Community | PMF, 50 or more customers, retention is the constraint, an owner | 12 to 24 months | 10 to 15 a week |
| Podcast | 7 of 10 buyers listen; a real host; email and social working | 12 to 18 months | 8 to 12 per episode |
| Creators | PMF confirmed, message set, a capture path | A pilot reads in 30 to 90 days | 10 to 20% of one person |
| Analyst relations | ACV above $25K, 10 or more enterprise deployments, stable story | 12 to 18 months | 10 or more a month |
| References | 10 or more customers (30 or more for a program) | Weeks | A few hours a month |

**Decision rules**
- If PMF is unclear at seed, then defer company social, community, podcast, creators, and analyst relations. They amplify a message you are still changing.
- If the problem is this quarter's pipeline, then none of these is the fix. Use paid (FW-DEMANDGEN-006) or outbound now and build these in parallel.
- If you have one marketer, then run one new channel at a time. Add the next after 90 days of data and an on-schedule first channel. A dark channel hurts more than none.
- If a channel needs more hours than you can protect, then do not start it.
- Seed order: founder LinkedIn, 3 to 5 speaking slots a year, references by hand, creators after PMF. Series A order: company LinkedIn, one webinar a month, an executive dinner pilot if the CEO has the relationships, then sponsored events with measurement. Series B adds community (if retention is the constraint), analyst relations (if ACV is above $25K), and a reference program.
- If the buyer is a developer, then LinkedIn is not primary. Find where they gather (X, Hacker News, Reddit, Discord, Slack groups, YouTube) and join in; do not broadcast.

**Where it goes wrong:** Starting three channels and finishing none. Choosing a channel because it is familiar and not because the ICP uses it. Launching before UTMs and CRM tags exist, so nothing can be judged.

## Social: platform, cadence, production, engagement, measurement  (FW-SOCIAL-001, FW-SOCIAL-002, FW-SOCIAL-003, FW-SOCIAL-004, FW-SOCIAL-005)

FW-SOCIAL-001 owns the platform decision and program. FW-SOCIAL-002 owns LinkedIn specifics.

**When this matters:** Prospects check your LinkedIn before replying and find a thin or stale page. You are Series A or later with an ICP and no owned social presence. Good content is not being distributed. Outbound replies are under 3%.
**Skip it when:** PMF is unclear. Conversion, not awareness, is the constraint. Nobody can protect 4 hours a week and produce 2 or more original posts a week. The buyer is a developer off LinkedIn. You sell only through partners.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Posts when someone remembers. No calendar or owner. Engagement under 1.5%. Links in post bodies.
- Tier 2 Building: A calendar and owner. 3 to 4 posts a week in peak windows. Engagement 1.5 to 3%.
- Tier 3 Operating: Pillars tied to buyer problems, 4 to 5 posts a week, engagement 2.5 to 4.5%, a monthly dashboard, a formal CEO routine, a newsletter.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Engagement signals feed lead scoring and account alerts. Employees share. Strategy is reviewed quarterly from data.

**First build, in order**
1. Pick the platform from the buyer. Put 70% or more of effort on one primary platform. Write down which platforms you skip. Stay at two platforms or fewer until the first works.
2. Complete the company page before posting: logo, cover, a 250 to 300 word buyer-facing description, tags, working button. (2 to 4 hours)
3. Name one owner and the weekly hours: 4 is the floor, 6 covers strategy, production, publishing, and monitoring. If nobody can commit 4, do not start.
4. Write 3 to 5 pillars in the buyer's words, each tied to a buyer problem.
5. Build a buffer of 8 posts (2 a week for 4 weeks) before going live. (3 to 5 hours)
6. Get the CEO to engage with 2 company posts a week (about 5 minutes). Send a Slack alert with a draft comment.
7. Add UTM tags to every social link, name social as a source in analytics, and record a baseline of followers, engagement rate, and sessions.
8. Move on only after 8 consecutive on-schedule weeks, a baseline rate, and a buffer kept 2 weeks ahead.

**Decision rules**
- If you are Tier 1 or 2, then 2 posts a week is the floor and 3 to 4 the target. Bursting then going dark is worse than steady weekly posts. (FW-SOCIAL-002 sets this over the 1 to 2 a week in FW-SOCIAL-001.)
- If you are seed with the founder as the only marketer, then post from the founder's profile 2 to 4 times a week on real expertise, keep the company page complete, skip tools, and keep every post to 30 minutes of work. Text only.
- If one person cannot protect 25 to 30 minutes a day for replies on top of production, then cut posting to 2 a week. Answered comments beat volume.
- If engagement falls for 3 months, then do not post more or buy ads. Sort posts by format. If one format fell, cut it. If all fell, check whether brand-broadcast content passed 35% and review the pillars.
- If you want paid amplification, then show 8 or more on-schedule weeks, engagement of at least 2%, and pillars first.
- If sales is outbound-heavy, then give SDRs a monthly kit of 5 to 10 posts and add "engage with the prospect's recent post" to sequences. Judge by social-influenced pipeline, not first-touch leads.
- If production keeps breaking, then audit before hiring. The cause is usually no visual template (a template cuts visual time 50 to 70%) or slow approval. Write two columns: what the owner approves alone, what needs a named reviewer.
- If a dark period happened, then restart at half cadence, rebuild a 4-week buffer, and fix the cause first.
- If the company is regulated, then build 30 to 50 pre-approved templates, send new ones to review in batches, and plan a two-week buffer.
- If you use AI for drafts, then start from a structured brief, edit to a checklist, and have a person approve. AI alone writes generic posts.
- If a negative comment arrives, then acknowledge within 4 business hours, correct a factual error once, and move to DM.
- If an ICP-fit person engages 3 times, then route them to sales. For named enterprise accounts (ACV above $50K), route the first engagement within 24 hours.
- If you have under 500 followers and under 5 comments a week, then skip an engagement system. Reply as comments arrive.
- If you can do one measurement thing, then add UTMs. If a budget review is 60 days out, start now: 60 days of data shows traffic, conversions, and social-sourced leads.
- If reach drops 30 to 40% across all posts, then check for an algorithm change, more link posts, a restart after a dark period (2 to 4 weeks to rebuild), and falling engagement. Do not change strategy on one month.
- If you wonder who to hire, then at Series A give 25 to 30% of a content marketer's time to LinkedIn. A dedicated role fits Series B with 80 or more employees and a program already producing results. No agency at seed or Series A; a part-time freelance writer ($500 to $1,000 a month) beats one.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate | Under 5,000 followers: 2.5 to 5%. 5,000 to 20,000: 1.5 to 3%. Over 20,000: 0.75 to 2% | Under 2% below 5,000 followers is the action line |
| Reach per post | 2 to 5 times followers (strong), 0.5 to 1 (average) | Put links in the first comment |
| Follower growth | 5 to 15% a month under about 2,000 followers | Under 2% means ICP is not finding you |
| ICP follower match | Above 40% good, above 55% excellent, under 25% wrong audience | Quarterly job-title export |
| Sessions from social | 3 to 8% of site traffic; visitors convert at 1.5 to 4% | |
| Newsletter | Open 35 to 50%; under 30% is a topic problem. 500 subscribers at month 3, 1,000 at month 6 | Launch at Tier 3 |
| CEO amplification | A CEO with 5,000 or more followers can add 30 to 60% to weekly reach | |
| Comment replies | Average under 8 hours, 85% of substantive ones answered. DMs 100% within 24 hours | First 60 to 90 minutes matter most |
| Proactive comments on target accounts | 5 a week basic, 10 or more full | |
| Warm signals to sales | 2 to 5 a month at Series A, 5 to 15 at B; 15 to 35% become conversations | Lintel guidance |
| Weekly hours | Seed 2 to 3, A 4 to 6, B 8 to 12, C and later 20 to 40 | |
| Daily engagement time | 15 to 20 minutes seed, 20 to 30 at A, 30 to 45 at B | |
| Time to pipeline | 3 to 9 months | |
| Total social budget | Seed under $2,000 a month, A $1,000 to $3,000, B $3,000 to $8,000 | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Posting often with no audience definition and follower count as the only metric. The wrong platform. A founder-only presence that vanishes when the founder steps back. Every post a product ad. Posting more when engagement falls.

## Sponsored events, event selection, and conferences  (FW-EVENTS-001, FW-EVENTS-003, FW-EVENTS-005)

FW-EVENTS-003 owns the return target. FW-EVENTS-001 owns which events to pick. FW-EVENTS-005 owns what to do once you are there.

**When this matters:** You spend $50,000 or more a year on events with no pipeline number for any. You judge each sponsorship pitch alone. A CFO asks you to defend the budget. You are about to make a first big commitment.
**Skip it when:** You are seed with under about $20,000 to $30,000 of event spend (do the minimum below). The motion is purely product-led. There is no CRM. The real limit is PMF or sales capacity.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Events by habit, no pipeline number, one blast email after.
- Tier 2 Building: A scoring rubric, CRM tags, a one-page brief and debrief, a 90-day pipeline report.
- Tier 3 Operating: Real-time lead tiering, timed follow-up rules, a renew-or-drop call per sponsorship.
- Tier 4 Compounding: A portfolio view, 2 to 4 anchor events, cost per pipeline against other channels, competitor tracking.

**First build, in order**
1. Build CRM event tags ("Event, [Name], [Year]") and train reps before the next event. (2 to 4 hours, plus 30 minutes per rep.) Never tag after the fact.
2. Write a one-page brief for every event over $10,000: objective, pipeline target in dollars, qualified-lead target, outreach owner, staffing, hot/warm/cold rules, follow-up timing, and renewal criteria. Write the matching debrief; fill it at 7 days and update at 90.
3. Define tiers. Hot: asked for a demo or meeting, or is in evaluation, and fits the ICP. Warm: fits and is interested, not evaluating. Cold: everything else. Staff enter the tier in the moment.
4. Work out loaded cost for last year's events: fee, booth, travel, per diem, materials, staff time. It often runs 1.5 to 2.5 times the fee.
5. For any fee over $15,000, match the attendee list to your ICP and have reps send personal meeting requests 3 to 4 weeks ahead. Check at 5 weeks and 3 weeks.
6. For conferences over $10,000 loaded, write an activation brief 6 weeks out: a problem-framed booth headline, a two-sentence qualifying opener, tier rules, staffing, outreach, any dinner, any talk, and follow-up timing. The whole system takes 8 to 12 hours to set up.

**Decision rules**
- Return target: divide pipeline influenced (opportunities with an event touch in the 90 days after) by loaded cost. Aim for 2 to 4 times at 90 days. Below 1 times is critical. Above 5 times needs a check for over-credit. This is pipeline, not revenue.
- At Series A the portfolio target is 2 to 3 times and under 1.5 times triggers a review. Series B is 2 to 4 times. Series C and later is 2 to 5 times plus a 12-month revenue view.
- If you cannot name a pipeline number for any past event, then stop signing new sponsorships. Tag, run a one-time look-back on the last 3 events (2 to 4 hours), and decide from that.
- If you have no rubric, then ask three questions: are more than 25% of attendees ICP, is realistic pipeline over 2 times loaded cost, and can you follow up on time? Any no means decline or attend without sponsoring.
- If you score events, then use these weights: ICP concentration 25, buying stage 20, pipeline opportunity 20, competitor presence 15, cost per ICP contact 10, strategic value 10. Score 80 or more: invest fully. 65 to 79: standard. 50 to 64: attend only or reduce. Under 50: decline.
- If an event scores badly afterward, then diagnose execution first: outreach done, qualified rate above about 20%, follow-up on time. Most poor results are execution.
- If hot-lead follow-up within 24 hours is under 70%, then fix accountability before buying more events. Report it weekly, give follow-up to SDRs, and escalate at 48 hours.
- If you are seed, then do not sponsor. Attend and take 3 to 5 speaking slots a year. Keep the budget under about $15,000, mostly travel and registration. A talk can bring 3 to 8 inbound inquiries.
- If you are a solo marketer, then run at most 3 to 4 events a year or buy logistics help ($5,000 to $15,000 per major event).
- If you want a speaking slot, then submit when the call opens (4 to 6 months ahead) with a challenge-led title that omits your company name. If rejected, ask about the paid speaker track.
- If a competitor outspends you at a key event, then do not match. Differentiate with a talk, a side dinner, or a distinct booth, or drop it if it is not an anchor.
- Anchor events (2 to 4) stay at the lowest credible level. Review one only if ICP concentration falls more than 10 points over 2 years.
- Before upgrading a tier, ask what it adds. A meeting room beats a logo for a pipeline goal.
- If the buyer is a developer, then score developer concentration and sign-ups, not MQLs. If you are regulated, weight ICP concentration at 30 and favor vertical events.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline over loaded cost at 90 days | 2 to 4 times. Under 1 critical. Over 5 check | FW-EVENTS-003 |
| Per-event scorecard weights | Pipeline multiple 35, qualified rate 20, follow-up timing 20, ICP audience 15, pre-event outreach 10 | Over 85 renew. 65 to 84 renew same or lower. 50 to 64 marginal. Under 50 decline |
| Qualified lead rate (hot plus warm) | 25 to 40%. Under 15% means wrong audience or a passive booth | Booth conversations, not badge scans, 30 to 50% |
| Cost per qualified lead | Under $500 strong. $500 to $1,500 fine. Over $2,000 needs a reason | |
| Follow-up | Hot within 24 hours (90% met), warm 48 hours (80%), cold 7 days. Under 70% is a process failure | |
| Pre-event outreach | 80% or more of ICP target accounts. Acceptance 15 to 30% warm, 5 to 15% cold. Held-meeting rate 70 to 85% | Lintel guidance: skipping outreach means 3 to 5 times less pipeline |
| Speaking-session leads | 40 to 60% qualify | |
| Series A spend | 1 to 3 sponsorships, 2 to 4 events, loaded $50,000 to $200,000, 15 to 25% of marketing spend | Lintel guidance |
| Series B spend | 4 to 8 events, loaded $200,000 to $700,000, 20 to 30% of marketing spend | |
| Conference dinner | 12 to 18 people, the night before, invites 6 weeks out. Return over 8 times for a dinner, over 5 for a roundtable, under 3 is a mix or follow-up problem | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Blaming the event when execution failed. Counting only the fee. Tagging after the event. A broad giveaway that draws the wrong visitors. Renewing by habit and never dropping one.

## Owned events and executive dinners  (FW-EVENTS-002, FW-EVENTS-007)

FW-EVENTS-002 owns the owned-event format ladder. FW-EVENTS-007 owns programs aimed at named accounts.

**When this matters:** Buyers will not visit a booth but will join a small peer dinner. Enterprise deals stall because the economic buyer has never met your leaders. You want to host the conversation in a new category.
**Skip it when:** You have under 25 customers to fill advocate seats. Your brand cannot draw a yes. ACV is under $25K. Nobody can run follow-up. The CEO knows no ICP executives.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: A dinner or two, no playbook, follow-up by automation.
- Tier 2 Building: A written playbook, a repeat format, personal follow-up within 48 hours.
- Tier 3 Operating: Dinners in 3 to 5 markets, an owner at half time or more, executive touches tracked in the CRM.
- Tier 4 Compounding: A flagship event with waitlist, analyst and press attendance, and sponsors covering part of cost.

**First build, in order**
1. Score viability. Brand recognition and relationship depth carry 25% each. Under 50 total means build brand first. 50 to 79 means a dinner only.
2. Map your top 20 to 25 named accounts and score your executive relationship at each, 1 to 5. High opportunity and low relationship is your dinner list.
3. Invite at twice the seats: 30 for a 12 to 15 person dinner. Aim for 6 to 8 non-customer prospects, 3 to 5 advocate customers, 1 to 2 category voices.
4. Send personal invitations 6 to 8 weeks ahead from the CEO or head of sales, not marketing. Three to five sentences.
5. Run 90 minutes: 30 of mingling, a two-minute welcome, one opening question on a shared problem, a close with no pitch. No slides, no demo.
6. Within 24 hours the note-taker sends the relationship owner 3 to 5 bullets per prospect. Within 48 hours the owner sends a personal email citing something that person said. No automation.
7. Read pipeline at 90 days. Run the format twice before planning anything bigger.

**Decision rules**
- If you have under 25 customers, then defer and speak at other events.
- If you are Series A and the CEO knows 15 or more ICP executives outside your customers, then run a pilot of 10 to 12 for under $10,000 and read it at 90 days before starting a series.
- If someone wants a summit first, then run two dinners first. A summit also needs 100 or more customers, enough brand pull for 40% or more non-customers, a 12-week build, and capacity for 40 to 80 new conversations in 30 days.
- If the plan includes a demo or company slides, then remove them. It is the most common mistake. The demo belongs in the follow-up.
- If warm acceptance is under 25%, then check who sends the invitation, whether the theme helps the guest, and whether you gave 4 or more weeks.
- If NPS is high and pipeline low, then check whether prospects are under 40% of the room and whether follow-up proposes a next step.
- If dinners hold 5 times or better for 12 months in one market, then add at most 2 new markets a year (each needs 8 to 12 weeks of prep).
- If a deal is at risk from product, price, or competition, then a dinner will not help and reads as desperate. If the buyer has just never met your leaders, it can.
- If ACV is under $25K, then skip a formal program. (FW-EVENTS-007 also cites $10K in its quick reference; use $25K.)
- If the CEO resists, then show the cost: about 4 hours per dinner, 16 hours a year for a quarterly program.
- If you are Series B with 5 or more late-stage enterprise deals a year at ACV over $75K, then add executive briefing visits. A good private room is enough.
- If you are in healthcare, finance, or government, then get legal review of format, guest list, and per-person spend first.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline over loaded cost at 90 days | Dinner 5 to 15 times (A), 5 to 12 (B). Roundtable 4 to 12. Summit 2 to 8, first summit 2 to 5 | Small samples; treat the top end with caution. Lintel guidance |
| Invitation acceptance | Warm 40 to 65%. Cool 20 to 35%. Cold 10 to 20% | |
| Attendee mix | 50 to 60% prospects, 30 to 40% customers, about 10% voices | Prospects under 40% weakens it |
| Cost per non-customer attendee | Dinner under $1,500 strong, over $3,000 poor | |
| Personalized follow-up reply rate | 30 to 60% warm, versus 5 to 15% generic | |
| Event NPS | Above 50 good, above 70 excellent | |
| Dinner cost | $6,000 to $18,000 loaded (A), $8,000 to $25,000 (B) | |
| Executive effect | Deals 15 to 30% faster; win rate up 10 to 25 points on touched late-stage deals | Needs CRM tracking of executive touches |

**Where it goes wrong:** A strong event with a weak invitation list. A pitch dressed as a dinner. Generic follow-up. A guest list of friendly customers. Skipping dinners and booking a summit.

## Webinars and virtual events  (FW-EVENTS-006)

**When this matters:** Everything you do is in person and nothing runs between conferences. Your webinars draw under 30% of registrants and get one blast after. You have content with no live format.
**Skip it when:** No CRM tags. No content capability. Seed with no ICP and no marketing hire. Product-led with no sales motion.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Occasional webinars, one blast.
- Tier 2 Building: A monthly series, a production standard, three-track follow-up.
- Tier 3 Operating: Webinar, panel, and roundtable formats, plus a summit.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Virtual and in-person run as one campaign calendar, with an on-demand library feeding leads.

**First build, in order**
1. Set CRM tags for virtual events. (1 to 2 hours)
2. Pick the first topic: ask the 3 busiest reps what qualified buyers ask that you lack content for, then check search demand and community talk. Use the topic that shows up in all three; if none does, use the reps' answer.
3. Write a minimum standard: platform, registration page with one qualifying question, four reminders (confirmation, 1 week, 48 hours, day-of), slide template, moderator sheet, two follow-up emails. (2 to 4 hours)
4. Run the first event within 30 to 45 days. Aim for 150 to 300 registrations, a 40% show rate, 10 or more questions. Do not polish first.
5. Split follow-up by behavior: engaged attendees (hot, personal within 24 hours), partial attendees (warm, email within 48 hours), no-shows (recording within 48 hours, then nurture).
6. Pull the 90-day pipeline report.

**Decision rules**
- If show rate stays under 25%, then fix reminders first: do the 48-hour and day-of emails send, do they carry real content and not only logistics, and does the time suit the ICP's time zone? A good sequence on an average topic beats a weak one on a great topic.
- If registration is low, then re-run the topic check with sales. If every topic is about your product, reframe each as the buyer's challenge. A pitch dressed as education trains people to skip the next one.
- If pipeline is under 1 times cost at 90 days, then check in order: CRM coverage above 80% of registrants, hot follow-up within 24 hours with a next step, and ICP share of attendees.
- If deals are stalling, then run a virtual roundtable: 8 to 12 in-pipeline accounts at a stall, no slides, the account owner invites. 30 to 50% should restart within 30 days. If over 30% do, make it standing.
- If you want a virtual summit, then show 6 months of a steady monthly series, one panel, and an owner for a 10 to 14 week build.
- If co-hosting, then agree first on whose platform hosts, who gets the full registrant list, and who follows up with whom. Prefer your own platform.
- If you are seed, then defer. Informal talks with your network are community, not a pipeline program.
- If an on-demand library of 20 or more recordings gets no leads, then promote, do not produce. Give each recording a search-friendly page.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Show rate | 40% or more good. Under 25% fix reminders. Under 20% weak topic or wrong audience | Lintel guidance |
| ICP share of registrants | Above 50% at Series B and later. Under 30% means topic or promotion is off | |
| Watch time | Above 65% | Drop-off often starts when the pitch starts |
| Live engagement | Above 40%. Under 20% means weak mechanics | |
| Hot-lead meetings | 15 to 30% book within 14 days. Under 10% means slow follow-up | |
| On-demand views by no-shows | 20 to 35% watch 30% or more within 30 days | |
| Pipeline over loaded cost at 90 days | Webinar 3 to 6 times (A), 3 to 5 (B). Roundtable 5 to 12. Summit 2 to 5. Under 1 critical | Lintel guidance |
| Cost | $200 to $800 per webinar unpromoted, $1,000 to $2,500 with LinkedIn promotion | |
| Annual budget | A $8,000 to $35,000 (12 webinars, 2 to 4 roundtables). B $25,000 to $80,000 | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Topics chosen by marketing alone. One blast to everyone. No CRM tags, so pipeline stays invisible. A pitch in the first half. Over-building before the first run.

## Field marketing and regional programs  (FW-EVENTS-004)

**When this matters:** You have regional sales teams in 2 or more markets and run generic national campaigns. Pipeline is strong at headquarters and weak elsewhere.
**Skip it when:** One central sales team serves all markets. You are seed or early Series A in one location. The motion is self-serve. The cycle is under 30 days and ACV under $5K.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: Regions get national campaigns and whatever reps build.
- Tier 2 Building: A written charter with market tiers, budgets, and regional pipeline measures.
- Tier 3 Operating: A repeatable kit tuned per market.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Dedicated regional marketers in tier-one markets plus central operations.

**First build, in order**
1. List active sales markets and the marketing each gets.
2. Tier the markets (1 full program, 2 periodic, 3 national only) and agree the tiers with sales leaders before designing any program.
3. Pilot in 1 to 2 markets with a quarterly executive dinner and one local event, tagged by region. Hold a weekly sync with the regional sales leader.

**Decision rules**
- If nobody owns support, then a quarterly dinner plus one local event needs 20 to 25% of one marketer. Pilot it if that time exists. If not, say a dedicated owner is needed.
- If the program is only events, then add an account-based digital campaign to the top 30 named accounts.
- If marketer and sales leader do not work together, then reset the working model with both leaders. Do not force programs.
- If a market's win rate is low, then favor brand presence (local talks, associations) and track win rate over 12 to 18 months.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline over loaded cost at 90 days | Over 2 times per market. Under 1 for 2 quarters triggers a review. Series A pilot 2 to 4 times | Lintel guidance |
| Named-account coverage | 60 to 80% of the top 30 per quarter | |
| Weekly sync held | Above 85% | |
| Ramp | 2 to 3 quarters in a new market | |
| Budget | A $30,000 to $100,000 across 1 to 2 markets. B $150,000 to $500,000 | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Treating a regional program as a small national one. Funding by request volume. No sales partnership.

## Community: build, operate, grow  (FW-COMMUNITY-001, FW-COMMUNITY-002, FW-COMMUNITY-003)

FW-COMMUNITY-001 owns the build decision. Social comment handling is in the social block.

**When this matters:** Customers already gather in unofficial groups and you own none of it. Retention is the constraint and churn comes from isolation. No practitioner community exists for your category. Word of mouth is your main channel with no owned home.
**Skip it when:** PMF is unvalidated. You have under 50 customers. Nobody can protect 10 or more hours a week. Pipeline is the constraint and retention is healthy. You cannot wait 12 to 24 months.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: No owned community, or a dormant Slack.
- Tier 2 Building: 25 to 50 founding members, a stated purpose, a weekly facilitated thread.
- Tier 3 Operating: Public and programmed, with lifecycle steps, health metrics, ambassadors.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Retention and referral tied to community in the CRM; members write most content.

**First build, in order**
1. Interview 10 to 15 customers (20 to 30 minutes each, 2 to 3 weeks): where do you talk to peers now, what do you get that search cannot give, would you be one of the first 25? Under 5 of 15 interested: stop. 8 or more interested and 5 or more willing to found: go.
2. Write the purpose in one sentence from the member's side, around a problem, not the product.
3. Choose one primary objective (support, product, acquisition, contribution, or engagement) and at most one secondary (the library calls this the SPACE model). Pick the platform after. Forum tools (Discourse, Circle) suit support and search. Slack and Discord suit chat but keep content poorly.
4. Recruit 25 to 50 founders one by one. Ask about 30 minutes a week for 90 days.
5. Seed before anyone arrives: a welcome post, a model introduction, 5 to 10 prompts, a one-page guideline, a welcome template for 48 hours.
6. Set a rhythm: two facilitated discussions a week on fixed days plus a monthly member spotlight. Write 8 prompts first. Make each answerable from experience in 2 to 5 sentences.
7. Build onboarding: an automated welcome with one specific question, a personal DM within 48 hours, a day-7 nudge for members who have not posted.
8. Track weekly: total members, new members, monthly active members, active rate (against total members), and 14-day activation.

**Decision rules**
- If you are seed with under 20 customers, then do not build. Join third-party spaces and run discovery only. Revisit near 50 customers once 10 or more want a peer channel.
- If the founder is the only candidate owner, then defer. Run the interviews now; they are cheap.
- If you have no owner with a protected 10 to 15 hours a week, then do not launch. A community with no owner fails in 60 to 90 days.
- If customers run an unofficial group, then contact the organizer and partner. Do not replace it.
- If active rate is under 15%, then interview 5 members before adding anything. Usual causes: a product-based purpose, no activation path, a stretched manager.
- If 14-day activation is under 20%, then check that the DM goes out within 48 hours (most common miss), the first ask is one easy question, and early activity is visible. The personal DM, specific ask, and day-7 follow-up typically lift activation 8 to 15 points in 30 days.
- If the manager writes most of the content, then cut manager posts to 2 a week and leave the gap for 30 days. If members do not fill it, fix activation.
- If threads average under 2 replies, then answer posts with a follow-up question to a named member. That adds 1.5 to 2.5 replies in about 30 days.
- If you are at Tier 2 with no ambassadors, then recruit 5 to 7 top contributors, define 3 benefits that matter to them, and ask each in person.
- If the manager is leaving with no playbook, then write a 5-page handoff this week: the 4-week calendar, notes on the top 10 members, the DM routine, platform access, report schedule.
- If you want to grow, then both gates must hold: active rate 20% or more for 3 months and activation 25% or more for 2 months. Otherwise growth adds passive members.
- Start with a member referral program (4 to 6 hours, first non-customer sign-ups in 30 to 60 days). Reward with recognition, such as a badge after the invitee's first post, not cash. Add search only if the platform is public and indexable. Run one channel at a time and judge at 90 days. For enterprise buyers, conferences and peer introductions beat search.
- If someone proposes paid member acquisition, then decline until Tier 4. If forced, test 60 days and measure activation, not sign-ups.
- If member count rises while active rate falls, then pause growth spend and fix activation. Resume after 60 days above 25%.
- Payback: protected revenue is (non-member churn minus member churn) times the combined ACV of community members. Divide program cost by the churn gap to find the member ACV you need. At $100,000 cost and a 10-point gap, you need $1 million of member ACV. Below that, retention alone will not pay for it.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 90-day success | 30 or more engaged members, 25% or more active, 3 to 5 members posting unprompted | |
| Monthly active rate | 20 to 35% healthy. Above 35% exceptional. Under 15% structural | Count against total members |
| 14-day activation | 25 to 40%. Under 20% broken onboarding. Non-customers 15 to 25% | |
| Month-over-month return | 40 to 60%. Under 35% means no reason to return | |
| Member-written content | 50% or more at Tier 3. Under 30% is a newsletter with comments | |
| Thread depth | 3 to 5 replies. Under 2 is a support queue | |
| Event attendance | 15 to 25% of active members | Casual formats beat polished webinars |
| Top-10 concentration | 30 to 40% or less of content. Over 50% is fragile | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Launching before enough customers care. No owner whose main job is community. A purpose built on the product. Chasing member count while active rate falls. Changing platform to fix an activation problem.

## Podcast economics  (FW-PODCAST-001, FW-PODCAST-002, FW-PODCAST-003)

FW-PODCAST-001 owns go/no-go and targets. FW-PODCAST-002 owns production. FW-PODCAST-003 owns growth.

**When this matters:** Your buyer listens to professional audio and no show serves their exact role. A senior person on your team is a strong conversationalist with real expertise. You want 45-minute talks with senior practitioners who would not take a sales call. Content, email, and social already work.
**Skip it when:** Buyers do not listen to podcasts. Nobody can host well. Content basics are missing. The goal is search traffic. You cannot commit 12 months. You are seed in survival mode.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: A concept idea, no pilot. Or ad hoc episodes with no checklist.
- Tier 2 Building: A concept, a vetted host, a pilot, a written production checklist.
- Tier 3 Operating: Launched with 6 episodes banked, a guest pipeline, a calendar 6 weeks out.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Measured from downloads to commercial impact, with defined roles and a review cycle.

**First build, in order**
1. Interview 5 to 10 ICP practitioners about listening habits. Go if 7 of 10 listen professionally and 5 or more like the concept. No-go if fewer than 5 of 10 listen.
2. Write a one-paragraph concept: who, what problem, format, why now, point of view. Name it for the audience or problem, not the company. At least 2 of 3 test listeners should say they would subscribe.
3. Test the host with an unedited pilot played for 3 outsiders. At least 2 should want another episode.
4. Write the production system. Starter gear is $300 to $700 (dynamic microphone, interface, headphones, a remote tool recording each side locally); hosting is $19 to $49 a month. Budget 8 to 12 hours per episode: booking 1, guest brief 0.5, recording 1 to 1.5, editing 3 to 5, post-production 2 to 3, publishing 0.5.
5. List the first 10 guests. Launch with 6 episodes banked and 3 recordings confirmed ahead.
6. Add growth one lever at a time, 60 days each: a guest amplification kit, 3 or more owned channels, search-friendly titles, a rating ask (aim for 50 Apple ratings in 6 months), cross-promotion swaps, an email offer, and paid last.

**Decision rules**
- If the founder wants a seed-stage show for demand, then redirect. At seed it earns authority and access to senior people, not meaningful demand for 12 to 18 months.
- If a strong show already serves the niche, then pick a narrower sub-niche it ignores.
- If you are a seed founder, then give only prep (30 minutes) and recording (60 minutes) and outsource editing and notes at $165 to $270 an episode, about $500 a month for biweekly.
- If you cannot hold cadence, then go monthly. No weekly until 6 or more episodes ran on time. Never publish late twice: pause 2 to 3 weeks and rebank 4 episodes.
- If there is no content engine, then build a newsletter first and add the show in 3 to 6 months.
- If downloads are flat for 3 months and guest sharing is under 40%, then rebuild the amplification kit and require it for the next 8 episodes before any other growth step.
- If subscriber conversion is under 0.5%, then do not buy reach. Fix the first 90 seconds, test two title styles, start the rating ask, and recheck at 60 days.
- If conversion is above 1.5% and downloads are flat, then reach is the limit and a paid test is fair: cap $300 to $500, use a tracked link, pause above $40 per new subscriber.
- If completion is under 55% for three episodes, then cut the first 3 minutes (long bios, sponsor reads, recaps) and deliver value in the first 2. Under 45%, have an outsider note where attention drops in 3 episodes.
- If you want proof of commercial value, then set it up before month 12: a tracked link in every episode, "podcast" as a choice in "how did you hear about us," and a question to your last 20 customers. Otherwise real impact is invisible.
- If a prospect is a guest, then tell the account executive when it publishes. It is a sales touch.
- If the audience is product-led practitioners, then run 20 to 30 minute episodes, alternate solo how-tos with practitioner talks, and push 60 to 90 second clips into their communities.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Downloads per episode at 30 days (narrow B2B) | Under 100 below bar. 100 to 300 emerging. 300 to 800 healthy. 800 to 2,000 strong. Over 2,000 top tier | FW-PODCAST-001; FW-PODCAST-002 shows lower early bands (50 to 200 in months 1 to 3) |
| Monthly download growth | 5 to 10% in the first 18 months. Under 2% after 6 months means distribution or concept | |
| Completion | Above 65% strong. 55 to 65% average. Under 40% serious | |
| Subscriber conversion | Under 0.5% fix first. 0.5 to 1.5% acceptable. Over 1.5% healthy | |
| Guest sharing | Above 50% within 7 to 14 days. Under 30% not activated. Each share brings 15 to 40 subscribers | Free; the best first lever |
| Cross-promotion swap | 30 to 80 new subscribers average, 80 to 200 or more strong. Aim for 2 to 4 a year with shows within 2 times your size | Outreach success 30 to 50% |
| Cost per new subscriber | Guest share $1 to $3. Swap $5 to $15. Paid boost $15 to $40 | |
| CTA click rate | 1 to 3% for a specific offer. Under 0.5% means generic | |
| Cadence on time | 95% or better. Under 80% is a capacity problem | |
| Cost per episode | Light edit $75 to $200. Standard $200 to $500. Full $400 to $1,000. Agency $800 to $2,500 | |
| Program cost per month | Seed $300 to $600. A $500 to $1,500. B $1,500 to $5,000 | |
| Results at 12 months | A: 200 to 400 downloads per episode, 1 to 2 customers name the show. B: 400 to 800, 2 to 4 customers a year | |
| Hours | A monthly show at Series A takes about 8 to 12 hours a month; each added episode adds 8 to 12 | |

**Where it goes wrong:** A show built around the company. A host with no real opinions. No distribution to build on. A cadence the team cannot hold, then a gap. Chasing downloads when the goal is conversations with the right people.

## Creators and influencers  (FW-EMERGING-007)

**When this matters:** Your buyers follow specific creators (LinkedIn practitioners, YouTube educators, newsletter writers, podcasters). Paid social is falling on cold traffic. A competitor gets creator coverage you lack. The product shows better than it reads.
**Skip it when:** PMF is not confirmed (a creator will expose gaps to a practitioner audience). ICP and message are not set. Buyers are C-suite in regulated industries buying through procurement, or ACV is over $250K. You cannot capture and nurture what a creator brings.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: No program, no creator list.
- Tier 2 Building: A few relationships and tracked pilots.
- Tier 3 Operating: Scored creators, a standard agreement, affiliate options, cost per customer tracked.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Tiered affiliate, sponsor, and ambassador creators with an owner and dashboard.

**First build, in order**
1. Build a map of 20 or more creators across LinkedIn, YouTube, newsletters, and podcasts, with audience size, comment quality, and fit. (2 to 3 weeks) Favor views per post or open rate over follower count.
2. Score each. Weights: ICP overlap times 3, engagement quality times 2, product fit times 2, content quality times 1, tone fit times 1 (maximum 55). Under 18: skip. 18 to 26: seed the product first. 27 to 35: one pilot sponsored piece. 36 to 45: affiliate or repeat sponsorship. Over 45: ambassador.
3. Pick 3 to 5 to contact, favoring micro-creators (10,000 to 100,000 followers) with strong ICP overlap.
4. First message: no sponsorship ask, under 150 words, cite something specific they made, offer something useful (a data point, early access, an introduction).
5. Run one pilot over 4 to 6 weeks with a tracked link or code, clear disclosure, a one-page agreement, and a claim-accuracy check. Let the creator control narrative and format. No scripts. Track clicks, trials, and pipeline at 30, 60, and 90 days.
6. Build the fuller program after 2 attributed pilots, a customer cost for at least one creator, a map of 20 or more, an agreement template, and a named owner.

**Decision rules**
- If you are seed with confirmed PMF and a practitioner founder, then the founder writes to 3 to 5 micro-creators personally. No budget, no program.
- If you are Series A, product-led, and selling to small business, then this is a high-priority channel. Start with YouTube tutorial creators and newsletter sponsors; affiliate fits because conversion is fast.
- If budget is minimal, then go in order: relationships, free product seeding, commission-only affiliate for creators who convert, then a paid micro sponsorship once affiliate data confirms quality.
- If you are one marketer, then cap this at 15 to 20% of your time and 2 to 4 micro pilots a quarter.
- If buyers are enterprise, then aim at the practitioner who champions you in the account, not at executive-audience creators.
- If the product is self-serve, then link straight to the trial. Each added step costs 30 to 50% of creator-driven sign-ups.
- If sponsored engagement runs 30% or more below the creator's norm, then your brief is too tight. Cut it to legal and factual needs.
- If one creator drives over 50% of attributed pipeline, then diversify; keep under 35%.
- If a program ran 6 months with no attribution, then pause new spend until every creator has a tracked link or code.
- Compliance: if a creator is paid, gifted, or earns commission, then require clear disclosure of the relationship, write claim limits into the agreement, and limit approval to accuracy and legal. Regulated industries need legal review of the brief and agreement first.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Creator cost per customer | Below paid social at Tier 2; 30 to 60% below blended cost at Tier 3 | Lintel guidance |
| Click-through | YouTube segment 1 to 3%. Newsletter 2 to 5%. LinkedIn sponsored 0.5 to 2%. Podcast ad read 3 to 8% | |
| Creator trial-to-paid | At or above the overall rate | |
| Creator-influenced pipeline | 8 to 12% at Tier 2. 12 to 20% at Tier 3 | Lintel guidance |
| Single-creator share | Under 35% of attributed pipeline | |
| Affiliate pay | At least $500 a month keeps effort up. Under $200 and promotion fades | |
| Approval time | Under 48 hours (72 regulated) | |
| Relationship length | Over 9 months at Tier 3. Under 3 months moves no trust | |
| Budget | 5 to 10% of marketing spend at seed and A, 10 to 20% of a person. 8 to 12% at B with half to one full-time person | Exclusivity adds 25 to 50% to a fee |

**Where it goes wrong:** Choosing a big name for the follower count. Negotiating rates before any relationship. A pilot with no tracking. A company-written script. Picking by size and not audience fit.

## Analyst relations  (FW-INFLUENCE-001)

This block covers briefing mechanics. The category story and program goals belong to FW-EMERGING-005.

**When this matters:** You sell to enterprise buyers (ACV above $25K), analyst research shows up in your sales cycles, and you are missing from it. Deals are lost where procurement favors analyst-recognized vendors.
**Skip it when:** ACV is under $10K. PMF is unproven. No analyst firm covers your category. You cannot give 10 or more hours a month.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: No contact, or one-off briefings with no tracking.
- Tier 2 Building: Target analysts named, their research read, a thesis-led narrative written.
- Tier 3 Operating: A briefing cadence, a tracker, a prepared reference pool.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Formal vendor evaluations with a dedicated owner and 6 to 12 months of prep.

**First build, in order**
1. Name 5 to 10 analysts. Start from the last 10 enterprise deals, won and lost: whose research did buyers cite? Tier them: up to 3 top, 3 to 5 next. (3 to 5 hours)
2. Read their latest research and write a one-page profile of how each frames the market and where you sit. (4 to 8 hours)
3. Write the briefing as a market thesis, not a demo: the thesis (2 to 3 minutes), your different approach (5 to 7), quantified customer evidence (5 to 7), market position (3 to 5), roadmap (3 to 5). (6 to 10 hours)
4. Brief the top 2 to 3 with the CEO leading. Skip advisory contracts for now. Total: 20 to 30 hours over 60 days.
5. Track every analyst: last contact, what they understood, open questions.

**Decision rules**
- If ACV is under $10K, then skip and spend the hours on demand generation or community.
- If PMF is unproven, then wait for 10 or more enterprise deployments with measured outcomes, a stable category definition, and a story that will hold 6 months.
- If you are Series B at ACV above $25K with no program, then start now with the 60-day path.
- If the briefing leads with features, then rewrite it to lead with the thesis.
- If asked "should we join a formal vendor evaluation," then know inclusion follows the firm's criteria. Check category revenue, enterprise deployment scale, and geographic reach, and defer if short.
- If you hold a paid advisory subscription, then use inquiry calls to ask a question and briefing slots for updates. Inquiries need that subscription; briefings do not.
- If the category is new, then brief adjacent-coverage analysts on the category thesis, not on vendor comparison.
- If you land low in a formal evaluation, then ask for the debrief and build a 12 to 18 month plan from it.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Starting effort | 20 to 30 hours over 60 days | Mostly CEO and marketing lead |
| Series B program | $15,000 to $50,000 a year, 3 to 6 active analysts, 0 to 1 advisory contracts, marketing lead at 20 to 25% | Lintel guidance |
| Series B outcomes | 1 to 3 positive mentions within 18 months; 2 to 4 documented deal assists a year | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Briefing before the story is stable. Pitching a demo. Using inquiry calls for company updates. No tracker. Blaming access when the story was weak.

## Customer references and advocacy  (FW-INFLUENCE-003)

**When this matters:** Deals stall at proof and you cannot produce a fitting reference fast. The same 2 to 3 customers carry every call. Promoters (NPS 9 to 10) exist with no program. Case studies are rare.
**Skip it when:** You have under 10 customers (handle references by hand and keep notes). PMF is unproven. You cannot tell succeeding customers from at-risk ones.

**Where you are**
- Tier 1 Reactive: 2 to 4 customers carry all requests.
- Tier 2 Building: An interviewed pool, a tracker, a request process with a 48-hour match.
- Tier 3 Operating: A segmented pool with a fatigue limit, case studies at pace, a review-site program.
- Tier 4 Compounding: Tiered advocates with benefits; speaking, content, and referrals run as a program.

**First build, in order**
1. Pick 10 candidates from three lists together: NPS promoters in the last 12 months, success-team notes, and rep input. Customers on two or three lists go first.
2. Interview the top 5 for 30 to 45 minutes: the before state, time to first result, one measured number, how they would explain the choice to a peer, a memorable moment, willingness to speak with a prospect, and anything off-limits. Record their words.
3. Build a tracker: customer, industry, size, use case, outcome, best quote, what they agreed to, last reference date, count.
4. Run requests through one owner: sales sends 4 fields (industry, size, use case, timing); the owner matches and confirms within 48 hours and sends the prospect a one-page brief. Reps never contact references directly.
5. Write the first case study (4 to 6 hours) with two customer touchpoints only: the interview and final approval.

**Decision rules**
- If you have under 10 customers, then do it by hand. Build a program near 30.
- If the pool is under 5, then interview the 5 happiest customers this week. You get a pool in 2 to 3 weeks with no infrastructure.
- If a reference is used more than twice a quarter, then rest them 60 days and interview 3 to 5 new ones.
- If promoters do not turn into advocates, then make the ask specific ("will you speak with one prospect for 30 minutes?") and send it within 7 days of the survey. It converts 3 to 4 times better than a general thank-you.
- If deals above $50K stall at proof, then match is the lever: same industry, similar size, similar use case.
- If you are product-led, then start with a review-site ask (G2 or Capterra) at the first activation milestone before building reference calls.
- If a case study takes over 6 weeks, then cut to two customer touchpoints and move internal review between them. Target 3 weeks.
- If reps contact references directly, then stop it; it is the main cause of fatigue. If speed was the reason, fix the 48-hour timing.
- If advocates get no benefits, then offer a CEO thank-you note, early feature access, and a newsletter spotlight. All free.
- If you plan a formal analyst evaluation, then prepare references early. Each takes 4 to 6 weeks to ready; fewer than 8 ready is the risk.

**Numbers that matter**
| Metric | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pool size | 15 to 20% of paying customers. 15 to 25 at A, 30 to 50 at B | |
| Fatigue | Under 1.5 reference calls per reference per 90 days. Over 2.0 is a risk | |
| Fulfillment | 90% or more matched within 48 hours. Under 80% means a small pool or slow process | |
| Use in enterprise deals | 25 to 40% at Tier 3. Under 15% means misuse | |
| Deal speed | 10 to 25 days faster with a reference | |
| Case studies | 1 to 2 a quarter at Tier 2. 2 to 4 at Tier 3 | |
| Promoter conversion | 25 to 40% do an advocacy act within 90 days. Under 15% means a weak ask | |
| Referral share of new revenue | 10 to 20% at Tier 3. Under 5%: check CRM attribution first | |
| Budget | A $5,000 to $15,000 a year at 10 to 20% of a person. B $20,000 to $60,000 at 25 to 50% | |

**Where it goes wrong:** Believing there are too few happy customers when nobody made a specific, easy ask. Adding customers with no outcome interview. One reference carrying every call. Reps going around the owner.
